Compared to RomVault & similar

RomVault and clrMAMEpro are full ROM managers: they target people maintaining large, merged sets—directory rules, scan/fix levels, many archive formats (e.g. TorrentZip, 7z), CHD tooling, and (for RomVault) extras such as DATVault for automated DAT updates and deep wiki-style setup. Linux users often run RomVault under Mono; there is no official native macOS app (typical workarounds are Docker or other indirect setups).

Where RetroCheck fits better

  • Native builds for Windows, macOS, and Linux from one project—no Mono layer and no Windows-only GUI as the primary path.
  • Minimal workflow: load a DAT, choose a folder, scan. Built for verification and classification, not rebuilding entire sets or tuning cache/archive pipelines.
  • Lower overhead when you only need to know what matches the database, what’s wrong, and what’s missing—not full set repair or multi-DAT merge workflows.

What you give up

  • RetroCheck does not replace RomVault-style fixing/rebuilding, integrated DAT subscriptions, or the breadth of archive and CHD features those tools provide.
  • Other modern cross-platform managers (e.g. Subrom) offer full library dashboards and deep workflows; RetroCheck is intentionally smaller in scope.

Use RomVault or clrMAMEpro when you need pro-grade set maintenance; use RetroCheck when you want a straightforward cross-platform check against a DAT.
